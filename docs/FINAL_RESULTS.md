# Final Results

## Research question and hypotheses

The study asks whether time-aware market regimes can be identified from daily SPY information and whether abnormal SPY trading volume adds useful information for predicting future regime transitions. The hypotheses were:

1. Market regimes differ in return, conditional volatility, and drawdown.
2. Abnormal volume is associated with transitions.
3. Abnormal volume improves prediction of future transitions beyond market features, VIX, and the current filtered regime.

The evidence distinguishes description, statistical association, prediction, and causation. This observational study does not establish causation or trading profitability.

## Data and design

The sample uses SPY and ^VIX observations from 2000-01-01 through 2026-05-31. The eligible final sample contains 6,390 observations from 2000-12-29 through 2026-05-29. Training covers 2000-12-29 through 2017-12-29 (4,277 observations); the untouched test period covers 2018-01-02 through 2026-05-29 (2,113 observations). The five-day primary target uses 4,272 training rows and 2,108 test rows after complete-window requirements.

## Features

Adjusted close defines simple return, log return, and rolling drawdown. The primary regime vector is `(Log_Return, GARCH_Volatility_TrainFit, Drawdown_252)`. Abnormal volume is current SPY volume divided by the mean of the preceding 20 trading days, excluding today's volume; `Log_Abnormal_Volume` is its natural logarithm. `Drawdown_252` is current adjusted close divided by the highest adjusted close in the current 252-trading-day window, minus one. A 60-trading-day drawdown is a robustness feature. VIX is an external regression control and is excluded from the HMM.

## GARCH volatility

The finalized conditional-volatility model is GARCH(1,1) with Student-t innovations, fitted on the training period and recursively carried forward. It converged on 4,527 observations with log likelihood `-6098.256165990207`, AIC `12206.512331980413`, and BIC `12238.601405717593`. The normal-innovation comparison had BIC `12421.279987399012`, so the Student-t specification had the lower BIC. The fitted parameters were `mu=0.07547480110100613`, `omega=0.00936885583182522`, `alpha=0.10653228087113768`, `beta=0.8924624978229001`, and `nu=6.341358396405996`.

## K-means baseline

The revised K-means model is a non-temporal, matched-feature baseline. It uses the same three regime features as the HMM, with a scaler fitted on training data only. The selected baseline has K=3. It provides a similarity-based comparison; it does not model persistence or transition dynamics and is not the primary regime model.

## Selected HMM

Candidate models with K=2 through K=5, diagonal and full covariance structures, and multiple initializations were compared. The accepted primary model is a four-state diagonal-covariance Gaussian HMM, fitted on training data only. Its BIC is `9626.339460105575`. Selection considered BIC, convergence, initialization stability, state sizes, persistence, durations, and economic interpretability. The selected reconstruction used saved Stage 10B parameters and did not refit the model.

## Transition target

The primary target is `Any_Transition_Within_5D`. For forecast origin `t`, it equals 1 if any state change occurs on one or more of the next five trading observations, and 0 only when the complete five-observation window is observed and no change occurs. Dates without complete future windows remain missing. Training windows crossing the cutoff are purged. Secondary targets include stress entry, stress exit, acute entry, worsening, improving, and horizons of 1, 10, and 20 observations.

## Stage 12: volume association

The primary training comparison found no meaningful unconditional abnormal-volume difference. For `Log_Abnormal_Volume`, the transition-minus-non-transition mean difference was `-0.011363570253285694`, Hedges' g was `-0.034206812171484045`, and the Welch p-value was `0.4227816078612033`. The 95% moving-block bootstrap interval was `[-0.05218225868300364, 0.028535157710254555]`.

The training-period volume-by-volatility interaction was significant: the robust Type III interaction p-value was `3.6643864491195807e-11`, and the HAC joint Wald test p-value was `1.0473432232819184e-07`. The saved difference-in-differences intervals were `[0.10427118977756791, 0.254591676806953]` for low-minus-medium and `[-0.15840830584700216, 0.057429044716806925]` for low-minus-high. Secondary transition-specific associations were heterogeneous: for example, stress entry had mean difference `0.3204092569597774` with Welch p-value `1.3262873442807203e-08`, while stress improving had mean difference `-0.1509516945294241` with Welch p-value `5.913939259064022e-16`. These conditional and transition-specific results are exploratory and do not overturn the primary conclusion.

## Final conclusion

Abnormal volume did not provide stable general incremental out-of-sample predictive value for five-observation transition prediction under the tested specifications. The primary unconditional association was negligible, and the significant volatility-dependent training interaction and transition-specific patterns are conditional exploratory findings rather than a general predictive result. No causal claim, investment recommendation, or trading-profit claim follows from this analysis.

## Limitations and future research

Regimes are latent, model-dependent classifications. Results depend on the feature set, HMM specification, rolling volume normalization, horizon, and the broad equity-index sample. Daily data may miss intraday relationships; structural change may reduce stability; some secondary event samples are small; and insignificance does not prove an exactly zero effect. Transaction costs and a trading strategy were not evaluated. Future work could test other assets, higher-frequency data, alternative transition definitions, and prospective validation while preserving strict time ordering.
